    Terms on This Step

    Grid connection:
    the utility wires and equipment that bring power onto your property, sized for a maximum amount.
    Substation:
    a fenced utility yard that lowers high voltage power for the lines in your area.
    Feeder:
    the utility line that carries power from the substation along the street to buildings like yours.
    Service transformer:
    the box near your building that lowers the voltage for your tenant's equipment.
    Meter:
    the device that measures how much power your tenant uses.
    MW (megawatt):
    a measure of how much power flows at one moment.

    Step 2 of 4

    Why It Matters Now

    Data centers, electric truck fleets and heavy manufacturers need large amounts of power, and they need it soon. When the local substation has no room left, new customers wait in line while the utility builds more. That wait can take years.

    • 1Data centerneeds about 30 MW
    • 2Truck charging depotneeds about 8 MW
    • 3Factory expansionneeds about 15 MW
    • Typical wait for new serviceseveral years

    A site that can deliver power now, instead of in several years, is worth more to these businesses. Your idle capacity is that kind of site.

    Customer sizes are examples.

    Terms on This Step

    Large load:
    a single customer that needs several MW or more, such as a data center.
    Utility upgrade:
    new lines or substation equipment the utility must build before it can serve a large new customer.

    Terms on This Step

    Battery storage:
    rows of batteries in cabinets the size of shipping containers that store power and release it later.
    MWh (megawatt hour):
    an amount of energy, equal to 1 MW flowing for 1 hour.
    Inverter:
    equipment that converts battery power into the kind the grid uses, and back again.
    Pad transformer:
    raises the battery power to the voltage of the utility line.
    Peak:
    the hours, usually late afternoon and evening, when the area uses the most power.

    Step 4 of 4

    What You Get

    You lease Stage the corner. Stage builds, owns and runs the equipment. You collect three kinds of income.

    Option payment

    $15,000 per year

    Paid during the 24 month development period while Stage secures permits and utility approval. About $30,000 over the 24 months.

    Ground rent

    $130,000 per year

    $13 per sq ft per year on the pad of about 10,000 sq ft, once the batteries are built.

    Power upside

    50% share

    If a high power tenant signs on to use this power, you receive half of the value that power creates. The amount depends on that tenant's deal.
